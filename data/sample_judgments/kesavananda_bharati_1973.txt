Case Title: Kesavananda Bharati Sripadagalvaru and Ors. v. State of Kerala and Anr.
Court: Supreme Court of India
Document ID: SCI-1973-WP-135
Year: 1973
Date of Decision: 24 April 1973
Bench: S.M. Sikri C.J., J.M. Shelat, K.S. Hegde, A.N. Grover, A.N. Ray, P. Jaganmohan Reddy, D.G. Palekar, H.R. Khanna, K.K. Mathew, M.H. Beg, S.N. Dwivedi, A.K. Mukherjea, Y.V. Chandrachud JJ.
Source: AIR 1973 SC 1461; (1973) 4 SCC 225

JUDGMENT:
Para 1. The primary constitutional question presented before this Full Bench of thirteen Judges pertains to the constitutional validity of the Kerala Land Reforms Act, 1963, as amended in 1969 and 1971, and the scope of the amending power of Parliament under Article 368 of the Constitution of India following the Constitution (Twenty-fourth Amendment) Act, 1971, the Constitution (Twenty-fifth Amendment) Act, 1971, and the Constitution (Twenty-ninth Amendment) Act, 1972.

Para 2. The petitioner, as the head of the Edneer Mutt in Kasaragod district of Kerala, challenged the state land reform laws that placed restrictions on the ownership and management of property held by the religious mutt under Article 26 of the Constitution.

Para 3. The Union of India contended that the amending power of Parliament under Article 368 is plenary, sovereign, and without any substantive limitations whatsoever. It was argued that any provision of the Constitution could be amended, repealed, or abridged by Parliament exercising its constituent power.

Para 4. The petitioners, represented by Senior Counsel Nani Palkhivala, contended that the word "amendment" in Article 368 implies the preservation of the original identity and framework of the Constitution. The constituent power cannot be exercised to abrogate the core principles and democratic values upon which the Republic is founded.

Para 5. By a majority of 7 to 6, this Court enunciates the Basic Structure Doctrine. It is held that while Parliament possesses broad constituent powers under Article 368 to amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights, this power is not limitless. Parliament cannot alter, destroy, or emasculate the basic structure or essential framework of the Constitution.

Para 6. The basic structure of the Indian Constitution encompasses: the supremacy of the Constitution; republican and democratic form of government; secular character of the Constitution; separation of powers between the legislature, the executive, and the judiciary; and the federal character of the Constitution.

Para 7. Consequently, Article 31C as introduced by the Twenty-fifth Amendment, to the extent that it barred judicial review by declaring that no law containing a declaration giving effect to directive principles shall be called in question in any court, is declared unconstitutional and void, as judicial review is an integral part of the basic structure.
