Case Title: Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India and Anr.
Court: Supreme Court of India
Document ID: SCI-1978-WP-231
Year: 1978
Date of Decision: 25 January 1978
Bench: M.H. Beg C.J., Y.V. Chandrachud, P.N. Bhagwati, V.R. Krishna Iyer, N.L. Untwalia, S. Murtaza Fazal Ali, P.S. Kailasam JJ.
Source: AIR 1978 SC 597; (1978) 1 SCC 248

JUDGMENT:
Para 1. The petitioner Maneka Gandhi was issued a passport on 1 June 1976 under the Passports Act, 1967. By an order dated 2 July 1977, the Regional Passport Officer, New Delhi, directed the petitioner to surrender her passport within seven days, stating that the Government of India had decided to impound her passport under Section 10(3)(c) in public interest.

Para 2. When the petitioner requested the reasons for impounding her passport, the Ministry of External Affairs declined to furnish them, asserting that the government considered it necessary in the interest of the general public not to disclose the reasons. The petitioner filed a writ petition under Article 32 challenging the action as violative of Articles 14, 19, and 21 of the Constitution.

Para 3. The primary issue is whether the right to go abroad is part of personal liberty under Article 21, and whether an order impounding a passport without providing reasons or an opportunity to be heard violates procedural due process and natural justice.

Para 4. This Court holds that the expression "personal liberty" in Article 21 is of the widest amplitude and encompasses a variety of rights which go to constitute the personal liberty of man, including the right to travel abroad as previously recognized in Satwant Singh Sawhney v. D. Ramarathnam (1967).

Para 5. The procedure contemplated by Article 21 cannot be arbitrary, oppressive, or fanciful; it must satisfy the requirements of being just, fair, and reasonable. The doctrine of procedural fairness and the principles of natural justice (audi alteram partem) are implicit in Article 21.

Para 6. Articles 14, 19, and 21 are not mutually exclusive watertight compartments. They form a golden triangle of constitutional protection. A law depriving a person of personal liberty must not only satisfy Article 21, but must also satisfy the test of non-arbitrariness under Article 14 and the reasonable restrictions under Article 19.

Para 7. Although the impugned order failed to afford a prior hearing, the Attorney General's assurance that the petitioner would be provided an expeditious post-decisional hearing and consideration of representation cured the immediate defect, rendering quashing of the order unnecessary.
